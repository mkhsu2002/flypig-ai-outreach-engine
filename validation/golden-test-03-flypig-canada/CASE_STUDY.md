# Golden Test 03 — FlyPig Uses FlyPig to Find Its Own First Adopters

## The question

Golden Test 01 started with a familiar consumer-channel problem.

Golden Test 02 started with an industrial market-entry hypothesis.

Golden Test 03 removed the predefined channel model entirely.

The product being promoted was FlyPig AI Outreach Engine itself.

The question was:

> If the engine is useful, who should actually care first?

## Why this test is different

There is no obvious category called:

`buyer of LLM prospect-research Skills`

The initial universe could plausibly include:
- sales teams,
- export teams,
- founders,
- consultants,
- lead-generation agencies,
- AI automation agencies,
- CRM implementers,
- market-entry firms,
- government export organizations.

A keyword search cannot solve the ICP question by itself.

## Mission v1

The first mission used a broad hypothesis:

`BROAD_ADOPTER_DISCOVERY`

The product was defined as an open-source, LLM-native process layer for prospect research and qualification.

The initial value hypothesis was simple:

> organizations that repeatedly research markets, build target lists, qualify accounts, or automate sales workflows may benefit.

The mission was intentionally wide enough for discovery to challenge it.

## What discovery revealed

Two types of organizations repeatedly showed much stronger workflow overlap.

### 1. Direct workflow practitioners

These firms already sell work such as:
- market-entry research,
- partner matching,
- outsourced prospecting,
- lead generation,
- territory/vertical research,
- international business development.

For these firms, the Open Core can potentially become an internal operating protocol.

### 2. Implementation multipliers

These firms already build:
- AI agents,
- CRM workflows,
- lead qualification,
- sales copilots,
- outreach/follow-up automation,
- guarded approval systems.

For them, the Open Core could become a reusable component in client implementations.

This evidence changed the launch model.

## Mission v2

The mission became:

`SERVICE_MULTIPLIER_FIRST`

The first-wave ICP was no longer:

> any Canadian company that might need better prospecting.

It became:

> service providers whose own work already contains prospect research, qualification, sales workflow design, or AI/CRM implementation.

This matters because a multiplier can use the Open Core repeatedly across clients or embed it in another workflow.

## Dedup became a real issue

Discovery found a related consulting/operator identity across more than one public site/brand.

The public contact identity and service language overlapped enough that treating them as separate prospects would risk duplicate outreach.

The engine therefore kept one canonical outreach record and preserved the relationship as an uncertainty rather than pretending to know the exact legal structure.

That is a more useful form of dedup than simple string matching.

## Audit challenged the phrase “AI agency”

Five initially QUALIFIED prospects were downgraded.

The most important pattern:

> AI capability is not the same thing as Outreach Engine fit.

Several firms clearly build AI systems, train teams, or automate workflows.

But if the public evidence did not show:
- prospect research,
- target-account selection,
- lead qualification,
- sales/CRM workflow,
- outreach/follow-up,

the adoption hypothesis was weaker.

Those firms moved to SECONDARY.

By contrast, AI consultancies with explicit CRM & Sales Copilots, lead qualification, CRM enrichment, AI-powered outreach, or guarded CRM automation survived Audit.

## Public institutions created another useful distinction

Two Canadian institutions were extremely relevant to the problem domain.

They publicly support:
- international market assessment,
- qualified contacts,
- partner identification,
- export expansion.

Functionally, they could benefit from disciplined prospect research.

But they were marked PASS for the current launch mission.

Why?

Because:

> could benefit

is not the same as:

> practical early outreach prospect.

Institutional governance, procurement and authority create a different adoption path from a founder-led consultancy.

The Open Core should preserve that distinction.

## Final result

20 organizations entered full research.

Before Audit:
- 18 QUALIFIED
- 2 PASS

After mandatory Prospect Audit:
- 13 QUALIFIED / SHORTLIST_READY
- 5 SECONDARY
- 2 PASS

The five downgrades prevented broad adjacency from becoming inflated product-market-fit claims.

## What the final 13 have in common

They fall into two groups.

### Direct adopters

Their current business already requires:
- market/partner research,
- target-account discovery,
- prospect qualification,
- territory analysis,
- outsourced business development.

### Implementation multipliers

Their current services already include:
- CRM/sales automation,
- lead qualification,
- sales copilots,
- AI-powered outreach,
- source-linked research,
- data validation and deduplication.

That is a much sharper launch hypothesis than simply targeting "companies interested in AI."

## What this test proves

It demonstrates that the single-agent FlyPig process can:

- infer a useful ICP from product/workflow fit,
- formally revise the mission,
- distinguish direct users from implementation multipliers,
- avoid assuming every AI consultancy is a strong fit,
- separate conceptual usefulness from practical outreachability,
- detect related-brand duplicate risk,
- and produce a concrete launch Tracker using the product itself.

## What it does not prove

It does not prove that these organizations will adopt FlyPig.

Public evidence cannot establish:
- interest in open-source Skills,
- current internal AI stack,
- willingness to change workflow,
- partnership appetite,
- budget for future paid execution products.

The Tracker says:

> these are evidence-backed organizations worth a first product/adoption conversation.

It does not say:

> these companies are customers.

## Why dogfooding matters

A prospect-research methodology should be able to survive a case where its own creator does not already know the target segment.

Test 03 did not begin with:

> find AI agencies.

It began with:

> who should care about this product?

The process produced a more specific answer:

> first target the people who repeatedly perform this work, and the people who already build the systems around it.

That is a useful product insight produced by the same workflow the repository is asking others to use.
