# Canada business-development prospect set for FlyPig AI Outreach Engine

## Market view

The strongest initial Canadian audience is not simply any company that does outbound sales. The more promising first targets are multipliers: RevOps and GTM consultants that can implement FlyPig for multiple clients, startup accelerators that can put it in front of many founders, and sales/GTM communities that can expose it to practitioners who already do prospect research and pipeline work.

I would therefore split the first approach into three motions:

1. Implementation partners: Canadian RevOps, AI and GTM consultancies already building sales workflows for clients.
2. Program and ecosystem adopters: accelerators and training organizations already teaching sales, AI adoption, commercialization or customer acquisition.
3. Distribution and amplification partners: communities and events with concentrated access to Canadian founders, SDRs, RevOps teams and SaaS operators.

The list below is the first practical prospect set I would actually work from.

## Who I would approach first

If resources are limited, I would begin with six conversations or pilot attempts:

1. Option 1
2. SpecSavi
3. Axiss.io
4. New Ventures BC
5. Communitech
6. Venture for Canada

Those six give FlyPig a balanced test across two important hypotheses: whether professional implementers find the open-source system reusable, and whether startup programs find it teachable and useful for founders.

Immediately behind them, I would work GTM North, League of Innovators and Startup Canada as amplification channels. They could create much broader Canadian awareness once there is a small amount of practitioner proof.

Martal is highly relevant to the problem, but it already markets a proprietary agentic AI lead-generation and AI SDR platform that identifies, qualifies and engages prospects. It is therefore more likely to view FlyPig through a competitive or internal-build lens than as an obvious adoption partner. Source: https://martal.ca/ai-sales-platform/

MapleSeedz, Makavele and Coyne.AI also operate directly in AI-powered outbound, prospect research or done-for-you outreach. MaRS has exceptional reach, with more than 1,200 science and tech ventures in its network, but based on the public evidence reviewed I found less immediate, explicit fit with AI-assisted prospect research than the accelerator programs above. I would keep it on the second-pass ecosystem list rather than the first outreach queue. Source: https://www.marsdd.com/startups/

## Practical BD implication

The open-source positioning changes the pitch. For the first Canadian targets, I would not lead with "buy our outreach tool." I would lead with one of three propositions depending on the target:

- Implementers: inspect it, adapt it, and use it as a reusable building block in client work.
- Accelerators and training programs: use it as a free practical lab for AI-assisted prospect research and qualification.
- Communities: let practitioners examine a real open-source workflow and debate or improve it.

That positioning gives FlyPig a better chance of earning adoption and contribution before trying to monetize the surrounding knowledge, implementation, support or advanced capabilities.
