## Broad research approach actually used

The research began broadly across Canadian organizations related to:

- RevOps and fractional RevOps
- B2B sales outsourcing and lead generation
- AI sales and GTM engineering
- startup accelerators and incubators
- founder communities
- sales and GTM communities
- AI adoption programs
- sales and AI training programs
- SaaS events and ecosystem organizations

The initial framing considered direct users, adopters, implementation partners, and distribution/amplification partners.

As evidence accumulated, the emphasis shifted toward organizations that could multiply adoption across many companies or practitioners, especially RevOps/GTM consultancies, accelerators, and practitioner communities. Direct AI-SDR vendors were generally moved down in priority because of greater product overlap and the likelihood that they already had proprietary internal systems.

## Approximate organizations considered

Approximately 30 distinct companies, programs, communities, accelerators, agencies, or ecosystem organizations surfaced or were considered during the research.

The final recommended set contained 15 prospects.

## Reasoning changes that actually occurred

The main reasoning change was a shift away from treating the market primarily as a list of companies that might use an outbound tool directly.

After finding multiple Canadian organizations already teaching or implementing AI-assisted sales, RevOps, prospecting, customer acquisition, or GTM workflows, the preferred entry strategy became:

1. implementation partners that could reuse an open-source Skill system across clients;
2. accelerators/training programs that could distribute it to cohorts;
3. concentrated GTM communities that could amplify it to practitioners.

This was not based on a formal scoring model. No numerical scoring procedure was performed.

## Candidates reconsidered, rejected, or deprioritized

The following were considered but not placed in the main first recommended set, or were explicitly deprioritized:

- Martal: highly relevant but operates a proprietary agentic AI lead-generation / AI SDR platform, creating strong competitive overlap.
- MapleSeedz: directly operates an AI-powered outbound and RevOps model, creating significant overlap.
- Makavele: sells a managed AI BDR / outreach system that already researches prospects and writes outreach.
- Coyne.AI: runs done-for-you outbound including list building and personalized outreach.
- DevCommX: offers AI sales systems and fractional RevOps; relevant, but with substantial service overlap.
- RevOps Co-op: highly relevant practitioner audience but global rather than Canada-specific, and its published community rules restrict promotion.
- MaRS: very large Canadian innovation network, but less explicit immediate evidence of fit with AI-assisted prospect research than the programs ultimately prioritized.
- Outsetly: relevant Toronto-based outbound and AI automation provider, but smaller and more directly overlapping than the selected first-wave implementation partners.

## Important limitations

- Research relied on current publicly accessible web information.
- Official organization/company sources were preferred where practical, but not every candidate had equally detailed public team or contact information.
- No private LinkedIn data, paid databases, private communities, private company information, or user-connected business data were used.
- Public evidence can establish current positioning and programs, but it cannot establish willingness to partner, budget, internal technical architecture, procurement constraints, or actual interest in FlyPig.
- No outreach was sent and no organization was contacted.
- No repository methodology was used to generate or refine the prospect decisions.

## Why research was considered sufficient

Research was stopped when there was enough repeated market evidence to support a practical first set across three distinct channels: implementation partners, program/ecosystem adopters, and distribution communities. At that point there were multiple high-fit Canadian examples in each channel, clear evidence of direct overlap with AI-assisted sales or GTM work, and enough lower-priority candidates to distinguish stronger targets from more competitive or less-direct alternatives.

No post-hoc scoring, audit, validation framework, state-transition process, or FlyPig-specific methodology was added after the research.
