Experiment 02 · Germany Industrial Water

The client asked for distributors. The market said that was only part of the answer.

A Canadian industrial water-monitoring supplier wanted a route into Germany. Instead of blindly searching for distributors, FlyPig treated “distributor” as a starting hypothesis and tested who actually influences equipment selection and deployment.

Mission changeDISTRIBUTOR_FIRST → CHANNEL_PLUS_INTEGRATION

The market-entry model expanded to include multi-brand distributor/integrators, water-automation integrators, and turnkey engineering / EPC firms.

01

The business problem

In industrial markets, the company that buys, specifies, integrates, commissions, or services the equipment may not be the same company that simply resells it.

Assumption

“We normally work with distributors.”

That is a useful starting point — but it can also trap the entire search inside the wrong channel model.

Market reality

Integrators control specifications

Water and wastewater integrators may select sensors, design MSR / PLC / SCADA systems, and commission the field equipment.

False positive

Technical relevance can still mean “competitor”

Some of the most relevant search results were German sensor manufacturers producing overlapping measurement stacks themselves.

03

How the shortlist was narrowed

20 organizations were fully researched. The biggest cuts came from distinguishing “technically relevant” from “commercially useful.”

Five of the 12 initially qualified companies were downgraded after deeper review — because evidence of external-sensor adoption, sourcing focus, or new-vendor fit was weaker than it first appeared.

04

The business takeaway

If you tell an AI “find distributors,” it can give you distributors. The more valuable question is whether distributors are actually the best route into that market.

In this case, the strongest research outcome was not a better distributor list. It was a better model of how the German industrial-water ecosystem actually works.

Important boundary
SHORTLIST_READY means “worth the next commercial step.” It does not mean the company has confirmed supplier interest, budget, certification fit, or purchase intent.
